## Topic
EN: Floor tells you to put the lid on (not locked) so players know the game is closed. Stay until the floor says "take your lid off." Floor removes the shoe, signs, cards, and vaults them. Then inventory one color at a time, biggest to smallest, starting with purple. Full stacks go on the incoming side, partials on the outgoing side; the peek area is a neutral zone with nothing in it. Never "patty-cake" (hand-to-hand transfer) — set down with one hand and pick up with the other. The nearest stack is broken down by 4s for purple so the floor can prove it. Dealer and floor must both know full-stack and partial values (class example: purple comes to $22,000). Closer paperwork is date / game / pit / table (BJ initials); both dealer and floor sign, and you are responsible for what you sign. A 25-cent error once pulled in security, the pit box, New York State Racing and Wagering, and the Gaming Commission.
ZH: 楼面让你先把盖子盖上（先不锁），客人一看就知道这台关了。等楼面说 take your lid off；楼面会收走牌靴、指示牌和牌并入库。然后按颜色从大到小盘点，从紫色开始：满垛放 incoming 侧，零垛放 outgoing 侧，peek 区是中立区什么都不能放。禁止 patty-cake（手接手传筹码），要一手放下再另一手拿。靠自己最近的那垛紫色按 4 拆开给楼面证明。经销商和楼面都要会算满垛/零垛（课堂例：紫色共 $22,000）。关台单要核对日期、游戏、坑、台号（BJ），双方签字，签了就要负责。曾经差 25 美分就要叫安保、收银、纽约州赛马博彩局和博彩委员会。

## Key lines
- [00:00] "Your floor is going to tell you to bring the lid on… We're not going to lock it. We're just going to put the lid on." Reason: if there is no lid, a player will sit down and you have an awkward "sorry, I'm closed."
- [00:47] "When you close your game… your floor is not standing here watching everything… we might be actually closing three or four games at a time." "You're kind of on your own, and your floor's going to come to you, and you're going to go through it together, each color."
- [01:12] "Stand here until your floor says, take off your lid." Floor takes the shoe, the signs from the shoe box, locks it, takes the cards and vaults them.
- [01:45] "Bring out your purple… We have to count every single color, and we have to pull out one color at a time… from the biggest color down to the smallest color."
- [02:04] "This is what we call… incoming side… This is your outgoing side. And your peeker is the neutral zone. Nothing goes there. Full stacks are going to go on your incoming side. Partial stacks are going to go on your outgoing side."
- [02:22] "You can't patty cake… when you go from one hand to another… You will get in a lot of trouble for this… There's a lot of things that people can cheat when you go hand to hand." "If you want to use two hands, you set it down with one hand and grab it with the other."
- [03:35] "The stack closest to you, you're always going to break down… The purple, we break down by four." Wait for the floor; don't call them over. Floor: "OK, stack that up… we have to prove this one."
- [04:21] "You got to know what full stacks are worth, what partial stacks are worth." Class count of purple lands on $22,000 (a full purple stack is $10,000 — 20 chips × $500, counted by 4s).
- [04:57] Paperwork: "They have the date, game, pit, and table… Initials for this game is BJ." "You're going to sign it and your floor is going to sign it. If you sign something, you're responsible for it."
- [06:01] Anecdote: a quarter used as a lammer by accident put the whole count off by 25 cents, and it brought in the opening dealer, the floor, the pit box, security, New York State Racing and Wagering Board, and the Gaming Commission.
